# Low-resource triage inputs: what the evidence will carry

The evidence separates sharply by *who* or *what* produces the input. Inputs that are physiological measurements made with a cheap device (oxygen saturation, blood pressure, arm circumference) have prospective validation in low-resource settings, outcome-anchored thresholds, and in one case a trial showing they catch deaths that clinical guidelines miss. Inputs that are lay interpretations of a symptom or a photograph are a different class: most are single-cohort, most lose accuracy when moved to a new population, and the ones that have been through a randomised trial have generally failed to change outcomes. The practical consequence for a triage pathway is that the referral decision should be carried by the measured inputs, and the self-reported and photographic inputs should be carried as context and as a personal trend. That is roughly the architecture already described on the Ayu hypothesis board, with two or three corrections set out below.

## Patient-reported input

Symptom checkers are the longest-running natural experiment in lay-facing triage, and their performance has been flat for a decade. The 2015 audit of 23 tools found appropriate triage advice in 57% of standardised vignettes, with the error concentrated in low-urgency cases, at 80% correct for emergencies but 33% for self-care ([Semigran 2015](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.h3480)). A direct five-year replication on the same 45 vignettes found median triage accuracy essentially unchanged at 55.8% in 2020 versus 59.1% in 2015, and the newer apps had become *less* risk-averse, missing more than 40% of emergencies ([Schmieding 2022](https://doi.org/10.2196/31810)). Two systematic reviews reach the same place from wider samples: triage accuracy ranged 48.8–90.1% across studies and was consistently better than diagnostic accuracy, which sat at 19–37.9% ([Wallace 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-022-00667-w)), and most included studies showed suboptimal triage accuracy with every study carrying at least one unclear risk-of-bias domain ([Riboli-Sasco 2023](https://doi.org/10.2196/43803)). Earlier work had already noted that algorithmic triage tends to be more risk-averse than a health professional's, which shifts the cost from missed illness to unnecessary demand ([Chambers 2019](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2018-027743)); one evaluation found that 51% of systems recommended more resource use than national guidelines did ([Ceney 2021](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0254088)).

The comparison that matters for a tool aimed at families is not against a clinician but against the person who would otherwise decide. Laypeople given the same 45 vignettes achieved 60.9% overall triage accuracy against 58% for 15 symptom checkers, and most participants outperformed all but five of the tools; the tools were better at detecting emergencies (80.6% versus 67.5%) and much worse at recognising when self-care was enough, wrongly escalating 24.7% of self-care cases against the laypeople's 4.1% ([Schmieding 2021](https://doi.org/10.2196/24475)). A follow-up analysis of the same layperson data found that people are overcautious about whether to seek care at all but still miss a substantial share of emergencies, and, most relevant to interface design, 79% of their triage errors were made while they felt certain of their judgement ([Kopka 2022](https://doi.org/10.2196/38977)). A family that is certain it can wait until morning will not open a symptom checker. This is an argument for an input stream that runs daily by default rather than one the user invokes when worried.

Two methodological findings should shape how any such tool is evaluated. Vignette-based accuracy is heavily tester-dependent: intertester agreement rose from 0.49 to 0.72 as instructions were tightened, and individual tester accuracy on the same system still ranged 43.9–57% ([Meczner 2024](https://doi.org/10.2196/49907)). And clinician-authored vignettes systematically misrepresent real use. Sampling vignettes from platforms where patients actually describe their own symptoms changed accuracy, safety, *and* the ranking of which tools performed best ([Kopka 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-83844-z)). An evaluation built on doctor-written cases will not predict field performance.

### Language models

The aggregate picture for LLM triage is mediocre-to-moderate with wide heterogeneity. Pooled accuracy in emergency triage was 0.51 for GPT-3.5 and 0.70 for GPT-4-class models, with no robust advantage over human triage ([Gao 2026](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12873-026-01521-y)); a second meta-analysis across 11 studies and 3,088 cases found pooled sensitivity of only 61% for the highest-acuity category despite 97% specificity and an AUC of 0.82 ([Cui 2026](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12873-026-01639-z)). Reliability is a separate failure mode from accuracy: across 10,980 simulated triages, repeatability of the same prompt accounted for 21% of total variation, and overall accuracy was 47.5% with a 13.7% under-triage rate ([Franc 2024](https://doi.org/10.1007/s43678-023-00616-w)). Where LLMs do well, the task has usually been reframed: given physician-written ED notes and asked only which of two patients is sicker, GPT-4 matched a physician reviewer at 0.89 versus 0.86 accuracy ([Williams 2024](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2024.8895)). That is a ranking task on clinical text, not a self-triage task on a patient's own words.

The consumer-facing evidence is considerably worse than the 13% under-triage figure currently on the hypothesis board. A structured stress test of ChatGPT Health across 60 clinician-authored vignettes and 960 responses found 52% of gold-standard emergencies under-triaged, including diabetic ketoacidosis and impending respiratory failure routed to 24–48 hour evaluation; when a family member minimised symptoms, recommendations shifted toward less urgent care with an odds ratio of 11.7 ([Ramaswamy 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-026-04297-7)). An independent audit in Japanese found near-universal hard under-triage of the primary recommended action in the red-flag slice — 100% under most decoding conditions — while the same model was appropriately conservative in mild cases, and tightening decoding improved reproducibility without improving safety ([Harada 2026](https://doi.org/10.7759/cureus.105878)). That anchoring result is the one to take personally. A tool that asks the family as well as the patient inherits exactly the bias that shifted those recommendations.

Two findings point to the mitigation. Running the identical benchmark against a retrieval-augmented, physician-facing platform cut emergency under-triage from 51.6% to 12.5%, and supplying objective clinical data eliminated emergency under-triage entirely, from 25% to 0% ([Jia 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijmedinf.2026.106687)). Giving the model measurements rather than only narrative is the intervention with the largest measured effect. Separately, counterfactual auditing shows LLM triage preferences differ at intersections of sex and race, which is a reason to audit by subgroup before deployment rather than after ([Lee 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s41666-026-00241-z)). Unsupervised patient use of a general model for triage was explicitly not recommended in a head-to-head against two symptom checkers and ED physicians, where GPT-3.5 produced a 41% unsafe triage rate against Ada's 14% ([Fraser 2023](https://doi.org/10.2196/49995)). The rules-first architecture already specified for Ayu, in which fixed red-flag rules decide referral and the model may add one but never remove one, is the design this literature supports.

## Measured inputs

### Pulse oximetry

Among all the low-cost inputs reviewed here, oxygen saturation has the best evidence that it changes who gets referred and who survives. In a prospective cohort of 3,848 Bangladeshi infants aged 3–11 months with suspected pneumonia, WHO IMCI guidelines without pulse oximetry identified only 25.0% of the children who went on to die as eligible for hospital referral. SpO₂ below 90% carried an adjusted risk ratio for death of 10.3, and a *failed* measurement carried a risk ratio of 11.4 ([McCollum 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2213-2600%2823%2900098-X)). A Malawi data-linkage study found SpO₂ below 90% predicted death independently of WHO danger signs, with risk ratios of 9.37 at health centres and 6.85 for community health workers ([Colbourn 2020](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1003300)). Most directly relevant to a referral algorithm, in 3,405 febrile children across five Asian countries, a model using simple clinical parameters reached 74.7% sensitivity and 99.1% specificity for severe disease against WHO criteria's 55.5% and 82.6%, and adding pulse oximetry raised sensitivity to 88.9% while cutting referral rates threefold, at an incremental cost-effectiveness ratio of $26 ([Chandna 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-026-04338-1)).

Respiratory rate is the other IMCI input with a device fix. Manual counting is unreliable enough that counting beads were introduced to help, with mixed results. They improved classification among illiterate CHWs but *decreased* counting accuracy among literate ones ([Noordam 2015](https://doi.org/10.1093/heapol/czu047)). Automated counters now perform substantially better: across 14 studies in seven countries, the ChARM device reached 95.8% sensitivity and 93.5% specificity, while the Masimo Rad-G ranged 75.9–95.4% sensitivity ([Ibrahim 2026](https://doi.org/10.7189/jogh.16.04281)). Neither is a phone, but both are cheaper than the referral they avert.

The caution that applies to all patient-measured vitals: when 300 patients collected their own vital signs with low-cost home devices, measurements were highly specific but sensitivity was poor for manual techniques, at 25% for elevated heart rate by pulse estimation and 60% for respiratory rate, while pulse oximeter and oral temperature readings held up ([Metlay 2024](https://doi.org/10.1089/tmj.2023.0548)). An automated device is acceptable; the patient counting is not.

### Blood pressure

The 140/90 red-flag rule is well supported, and specifically supported by data from the same Indian and Mozambican and Pakistani communities this work targets. A secondary analysis of 103,679 blood pressure measurements from 21,069 women in the CLIP trials found that neither elevated blood pressure nor stage 1 hypertension was associated with the adverse composite outcome; only severe stage 2 hypertension met diagnostic test performance standards, with likelihood ratios of 6.36 for maternal CNS events and 8.53 for stillbirth, and the authors concluded that current thresholds should be retained ([Bone 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2214-109X%2821%2900219-9)). Capturing an opportunistic cuff reading is worth doing; lowering the threshold to catch more is not.

Cuffless and camera-based blood pressure is a different matter. A 2025 state-of-the-art review concludes there is no compelling evidence that pulse wave analysis or pulse arrival time adds accuracy beyond the calibration cuff reading or the demographics used to calibrate, and recommends skepticism toward studies using uncertain testing procedures ([Mukkamala 2025](https://doi.org/10.1161/HYPERTENSIONAHA.125.24822)). Smartphone photoplethysmography is adequate for heart rate when the finger contacts the camera, at mean absolute error 2–4.5 bpm for contact apps versus 7.1–8.1 bpm for non-contact ([Coppetti 2017](https://doi.org/10.1177/2047487317702044)), but heart rate is not a triage trigger on its own. The board's "Promising" grade for phone-camera BP should move down.

### Arm circumference

Two claims are being conflated. MUAC measured *by mothers* for child acute malnutrition is one of the best-evidenced task-shifted measurements in global health. Minimally trained mothers using a colour-banded strap achieved over 90% sensitivity and over 80% specificity for global acute malnutrition and over 73% and over 98% for severe acute malnutrition, with weighted kappa 0.88 against CHWs' 0.91 ([Blackwell 2015](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13690-015-0074-z)). At programme scale in Niger, mothers were non-inferior to CHWs, detected cases earlier, and children in the mothers' zone were far less likely to need inpatient care, at annual costs of $8,600 against $21,980 ([Alé 2016](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13690-016-0149-5)). A 2024 Ethiopian study found caregiver-referred children had an 80.5% lower risk of severe MUAC at admission ([Tamirat 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/mcn.70054)).

Maternal MUAC for low birth weight is much weaker. In 928 Indian pregnant women, the best-performing cutoff was indeed 23 cm, but with sensitivity 54.0% and specificity 59.8%, barely better than maternal weight at 55 kg, and offered explicitly as a substitute for when weighing is not feasible ([Vasundhara 2020](https://doi.org/10.1111/mcn.12902)). "Works today" is right for the child-malnutrition use and too generous for the pregnancy use.

## Camera inputs

### Neonatal jaundice

BiliCam estimated bilirubin in 530 newborns across seven US sites with an overall correlation of 0.91 to total serum bilirubin, holding at 0.88–0.92 across white, African American, Hispanic and Asian American infants, and sensitivities of 84.6% and 100% under two screening decision rules ([Taylor 2017](https://doi.org/10.1542/peds.2017-0312)). The sclera-based approach sidesteps skin pigmentation by construction and was validated in Ghana on 336 infants: neoSCB identified TSB above 250 μmol/L with sensitivity 0.94 and specificity 0.73, against the reference transcutaneous device's 0.96 and 0.81 ([Enweronu-Laryea 2022](https://doi.org/10.1542/peds.2021-053600)). Those are the exact figures already on the board, and they hold. The underlying method subtracts ambient light using a flash/no-flash image pair, and achieved 100% sensitivity at 61% specificity in its UCL pilot ([Outlaw 2020](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0216970)). A Singapore multiethnic validation of a skin-based app on 546 neonates reported 100% sensitivity, 70% specificity, and a negative predictive value of 100%, but a positive predictive value of 10% ([Ngeow 2024](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2024.50260)). A skin-colour app reached r = 0.84 and 100% sensitivity at 69% specificity in Norway ([Aune 2020](https://doi.org/10.1111/apa.15287)).

The caveat the board does not yet carry comes from 123 darkly pigmented Botswanan newborns, the Picterus skin app correlated at only 0.66 against the transcutaneous device's 0.84, underestimated high bilirubin values, and reached just 64% sensitivity for TSB ≥ 250 μmol/L against the device's 95% ([Zimmer 2026](https://doi.org/10.1111/apa.70454)). Skin-based jaundice apps are not yet safe to deploy unmodified across Indian skin tones, and the sclera route has the better claim. Reviews of the wider AI jaundice literature report over 90% accuracy for neural-network variants but are explicit that these are assistive rather than diagnostic ([Salami 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.artmed.2025.103088)).

### Anaemia from photographs

The fingernail route has the strongest claim and a documented transfer failure that was then fixed. The original app estimated haemoglobin to ±2.4 g/dL with 97% sensitivity for anaemia, improving to ±0.92 g/dL with personalised calibration ([Mannino 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-07262-2)). Real-world data from over 1.4 million uses and 9,061 self-reported comparisons gave 89% sensitivity and 93% specificity at a 12.5 g/dL cutoff, with personalisation improving mean absolute error from 1.36 to 0.74 g/dL ([Mannino 2025](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2424677122)). In rural Bihar the out-of-the-box app was not accurate enough for screening, at ±4.43 g/dL in the clinic sample, and only reached ±2.25 g/dL after the algorithm was retrained on locally collected data ([Haggenmüller 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43856-023-00267-z)). That retraining step is a prerequisite, not an optimisation.

The eyelid and sclera route fails in exactly the target population. Across three years and 486 pregnant Indian women, smartphone colorimetry of the eye gave 89.6% sensitivity at 26.1% specificity, which is not clinically useful; removing participants with visible scleral pigmentation moved specificity only to 28.6%, and the authors attribute the failure to pigmentation prevalence being higher in pregnant Indian women than in the Ghanaian preschool population where the method had worked ([Wemyss 2025](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6579/adab4d)). The board's "Not yet" grade is correct and should be treated as near-final rather than provisional for this population.

### Retinal imaging

Smartphone fundus imaging based on indirect ophthalmoscopy achieved Cohen's kappa 0.868 against conventional fundus photography in 381 eyes at South Indian outreach clinics, with sensitivity and specificity of 0.79/0.99 for any diabetic retinopathy and 1.0/1.0 for severe disease, but performance varied sharply by device, with direct-ophthalmoscopy approaches performing worse ([Wintergerst 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ophtha.2020.05.025)). A systematic review of six low-cost devices found 52–92.2% sensitivity for any retinopathy and specificity of 95–100% for macular oedema, with the important constraint that every included protocol used mydriatic drops ([Prayogo 2023](https://doi.org/10.2147/OPTH.S416422)). That drop requirement rules out an unassisted home capture and keeps this an ASHA- or camp-level input.

## Audio

Cough acoustics have moved from promise to a field deployment in India, but the underlying evidence base remains methodologically weak. The largest synthesis, covering 89 studies from 34 countries, found sensitivities and specificities frequently at or above 90%, notably for tuberculosis, asthma and COVID-19, but only 11.2% of studies performed external validation and 71.1–87.6% carried high risk of bias under PROBAST-AI, with the authors attributing reported performance partly to small, homogeneous or crowdsourced cohorts ([Xu 2026](https://doi.org/10.1183/16000617.0288-2025)). A parallel review of 81 studies reports moderate-to-high accuracies across asthma, COPD and pneumonia and identifies "developing countries" as an emerging theme in the field ([Landry 2025](https://doi.org/10.1183/16000617.0246-2024)). In children specifically, classification accuracy for bronchiolitis, croup, pertussis and pneumonia ran 82–96% across five of six included articles, with a marked drop in the sixth ([Sharan 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijmedinf.2023.105093)). The croup work that anchors this reported 92.31% sensitivity and 85.29% specificity on a held-out set of 115 patients ([Sharan 2019](https://doi.org/10.1109/TBME.2018.2849502)).

The Indian TB case is the one to watch. Swaasa combined cough acoustics with symptom data in 567 subjects at Andhra Medical College, reaching 86.82% accuracy with 90.36% sensitivity and 84.67% specificity ([Yellapu 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-023-31772-9)). It has since been deployed prospectively to 7,879 people in Andhra Pradesh over six weeks with frontline health workers collecting recordings on smartphones; 780 were flagged and referred, 11 TB cases were confirmed for a 1.41% yield, and 36% of identified cases were asymptomatic ([Nammi 2026](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpubh.2026.1951831)). The asymptomatic fraction is the real argument for acoustic screening, because those cases are invisible to any symptom questionnaire. The yield numbers also show what a screening input is for. 780 referrals to find 11 cases is a reasonable trade at the population level and a poor one if presented to a family as a result. A contextualised classifier for TB, asthma and COPD is under prospective evaluation in rural Tanzania ([Isangula 2024](https://doi.org/10.2196/54388)). One comparative study is worth noting for architecture. An ML model outperformed 36 clinicians on respiratory sound samples (sensitivity 0.75 versus 0.67), but combining model and clinician predictions beat either alone at 0.83 sensitivity and 0.92 specificity ([Han 2023](https://doi.org/10.2196/44804)).

## Point-of-care tests and frontline algorithms

Where a cheap test exists, it generally outperforms everything inferred from appearance. HemoTypeSC identified 209 of 211 HbSS cases across 1,559 Indian newborns and adults, at 99.1% sensitivity and 99.9% specificity against HPLC ([Mukherjee 2020](https://doi.org/10.1093/ajcp/aqz108)); a later six-centre newborn screening study of 1,725 babies reported sensitivity of 93.3% for sickle cell disease with 100% specificity, and 96% sensitivity for sickle cell trait ([Thaker 2025](https://doi.org/10.25259/IJMR_193_2025)), slightly lower than the figure on the board and worth quoting as a range. A glycosylated fibronectin point-of-care test for pre-eclampsia reached AUROC 0.99 in 798 Indian pregnant women ([Nagalla 2020](https://doi.org/10.1111/1471-0528.16323)).

Digital algorithms that combine a few clinical signs with a point-of-care result have the best outcome evidence of anything in this scan. e-POCT reduced clinical failure from 4.1% to 2.3% against an IMCI-derived electronic algorithm in 3,169 Tanzanian children while cutting antibiotic prescription from 29.7% to 11.5% ([Keitel 2017](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1002411)); its successor ePOCT+, evaluated in a cluster-randomised trial across 40 facilities and 44,306 consultations, cut antibiotic prescribing from 70.1% to 23.2% with non-inferior day-7 clinical failure ([Tan 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-023-02633-9)). Smart Triage, a nine-variable model using age, heart rate, temperature, MUAC, oxygen saturation, parent concern, difficulty breathing, oedema and pallor, was derived in Uganda ([Mawji 2022](https://doi.org/10.3389/fped.2022.976870)) and externally validated in 11,595 children at AUROC 0.81, though it dropped to 0.62 in neonates and required re-estimation to reach 0.83 there ([Zhang 2024](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pdig.0000311)). Against ETAT guidelines in 5,618 Kenyan children, Smart Triage reached 74.9% sensitivity for admission and 79.6% for mortality against ETAT's 48.4% and 51% ([Kamau 2024](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pdig.0000408)). "Parent concern" earning a coefficient in a validated model is the single most encouraging result in this scan for a family-facing tool. An mHealth assessment platform for frontline workers showed 84–99% specificity correlation with local health professionals across 861 children in Burkina Faso, Ecuador and Bangladesh ([Finette 2019](https://doi.org/10.4269/ajtmh.18-0869)).

## What the trials teach

Four trials bear directly on the Ayu design, and three of them are null. The CLIP individual-participant meta-analysis pooled 69,330 pregnancies across Mozambique, Pakistan and India; CHW-led mobile-health-supported detection, initial treatment and referral did not reduce the adverse composite outcome (adjusted OR 1.17, 95% CI 0.90–1.51) ([von Dadelszen 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2820%2931128-4)). The Indian arm alone, 14,783 pregnancies in Karnataka, was likewise null (aOR 0.92), though women receiving eight or more contacts had markedly fewer stillbirths (aOR 0.19) in a non-randomised comparison ([Bellad 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.preghy.2020.05.008)). BUMP 1 found self-monitoring with telemonitoring in 2,441 higher-risk pregnancies did not bring forward clinic detection of hypertension — a difference of −1.6 days ([Tucker 2022](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2022.4712)), and BUMP 2 found no improvement in blood pressure control in 850 women with chronic or gestational hypertension ([Chappell 2022](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2022.4726)). AFFIRM, across 409,175 pregnancies, found a reduced-fetal-movement care package did not reduce stillbirth (aOR 0.90, 95% CI 0.75–1.07) ([Norman 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2818%2931543-5)). The CRADLE device trial recorded an 8% fall in the primary composite across 536,223 deliveries, but after adjustment for between-cluster variation the authors state they were unable to judge benefit or harm ([Vousden 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2214-109X%2818%2930526-6)). "Validated in pregnancy" therefore overstates it: the device is validated for measurement, not for effect.

The exception is the humblest intervention. Home-based records across 14 trials improved antenatal care, patient-provider communication and women's sense of control, and raised vaccination odds 2.39 for children and 1.98 for mothers, with no significant effect on newborn outcomes ([Magwood 2019](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0209278)). The record itself helps; the monitoring does not, unless it reaches someone who acts.

Two further corrections to the board's figures. The miniPIERS model was developed on 2,081 women with AUROC 0.768 and externally validated at 0.713 ([Payne 2014](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1001589)), but both subsequent LMIC validations are worse: in Zanzibar discrimination was described as inaccurate and unlikely to aid risk stratification, reaching 0.72 only in pregnancies before 34 weeks ([van der Meij 2021](https://doi.org/10.1002/ijgo.13493)), and in Guinea-Bissau AUROC was 0.664 with a calibration slope of 0.53 ([Turé 2026](https://doi.org/10.1002/ijgo.70850)). miniPIERS as a *structure*, covering parity, gestational age, headache or visual disturbance, chest pain or dyspnoea, vaginal bleeding with abdominal pain, systolic BP and dipstick proteinuria, remains a defensible checklist of what to ask. miniPIERS as a calibrated probability is not transportable without local refitting.

On depression screening, the board's EPDS figures hold exactly: pooled sensitivity 88.9% and specificity 93.4% at a threshold of ≥13 across seven Indian validation studies and 1,003 women ([Fellmeth 2021](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12888-021-03190-6)). Two refinements matter for a voice interface. Mode of administration changes the score. In 177 rural South Indian women, self-administered EPDS produced significantly higher scores and higher rates of antenatal, postnatal and perinatal depression than interviewer administration ([Badiya 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajp.2021.102890)), so a spoken agent is closer to the interviewer condition and its thresholds should not be assumed. And in a Hindi validation of six instruments in 480 women, the GAD7 and a somatic symptom scale had the highest AUROC among perinatal participants, with tiredness and body weakness the most commonly endorsed symptoms ([Fellmeth 2026](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2025-111111)), which fits the board's own observation that families describe function rather than symptoms.

## Gaps worth owning

Three holes in this literature are addressable by a team already collecting Indian field data. First, no validation exists for smartphone imaging of swelling as a pre-eclampsia sign, and none surfaced for tongue imaging outside unvalidated Chinese cohorts. Both remain genuinely unstudied rather than merely unproven. Second, every jaundice and anaemia photographic method that has been retested in a new population has lost accuracy, and the two that recovered did so only through local retraining; an Indian retraining dataset with birth outcomes attached is the asset nobody currently has. Third, and most consequential, the triage models with the best outcome evidence (Smart Triage, ePOCT+, the Chandna referral model) are all facility- or CHW-administered, and none has been validated when the inputs come from a family member at home. "Parent concern" survives as a predictor inside a clinician-administered model; whether it holds when the parent is also the sole data collector is an open empirical question, and the one a daily-log cohort is best placed to answer.

A closing caution on scope: everything here is screening and referral evidence. Sensitivity figures quoted for screening thresholds are not diagnostic performance, the positive predictive values in low-prevalence community use are low by construction, at 10% in the Singapore jaundice validation, and none of these inputs should be presented to a family as a result rather than as a reason to get a test. Decisions about any individual patient remain with a qualified clinician who can see the whole picture.
