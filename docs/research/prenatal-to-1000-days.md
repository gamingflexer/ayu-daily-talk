# Pregnancy to 1,000 days: what the 40 stories need, and what a tape can actually measure

Reading all forty stories end to end changes the shape of the problem. Twelve of them could be
materially changed by a low-resource input; eighteen could not be touched by one at all; the
remaining ten are mixed. Narrower still, only eight of the forty turn on a measurement that was
simply never taken — Cases 02, 06, 08, 10, 13, 22, 24 and 32. The eighteen fail on a record that did
not travel, an explanation nobody gave, a road, a stock-out, an Aadhaar gate, or a referral nobody
followed. Case 03 is a broken road. Case 19 is a
document gate. Case 23 is a vaccination card left with a grandmother. Case 39 is a newborn moved
between five hospitals with valid insurance. No camera, tape or sensor touches any of those. This
matters for sequencing: the input layer is where the interesting engineering is, but it is not where
most of the suffering in your own evidence base comes from, and your own white-space analysis already
says so — "no one gives the family the record" is a sharper statement of the problem than any
accuracy figure in this document.

The twelve that an input could change are Cases 02, 08, 10, 13, 14, 18, 20, 22, 24, 26, 32 and 35,
and they are not all the same kind of problem. Seven are a measurement that was never taken: blood
pressure in Cases 02 and 08, haemoglobin in Cases 10 and 32, sickle cell status in Case 13, a
jaundice look on day three in Case 22, and length as well as weight in Case 24. Three are a
measurement that happened but was not followed — feeding and weight gain after discharge in Case 18,
arm circumference after the nutrition centre in Case 20, and in Case 26 a newborn already measured by
phone video with no loop after it. One, Case 14, is adherence rather than measurement: the register
recorded that iron tablets were handed over, not that they were swallowed. One, Case 35, is
explanation: months of bleeding that nobody around her named as a symptom.

What unites them is that none needed a new sensor. Each needed an existing, cheap, validated
measurement or question to happen on a day when nobody was scheduled to be there. Cases 06 and 11 sit
just outside this group and are worth noting for why: Case 06 is a measurement problem but the
anaemia was also a nine-month treatment failure, and Case 11 is adherence and blood supply rather
than detection, so an input alone moves neither very far.

## The measurement question

You asked specifically whether a rupee note or a measuring tape could be used to measure the hand,
stomach or chest. The short answer is that the tape is strong and under-used, the stomach is the one
place it fails, and the rupee note belongs in a different role than the one you have in mind.

### Chest circumference

Chest circumference is the single best surrogate for birth weight in the literature, and it beats
foot length, which is where most recent attention has gone. A meta-analysis of 36,987 newborns across
24 studies found pooled sensitivity and specificity of 0.94 and 0.94 for identifying birth weight
under 2000 g, with a diagnostic odds ratio of 263 against 174 for arm circumference, a significant
difference ([Goto 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.respe.2014.11.002)). Pooling correlation
coefficients across 71 studies of chest circumference and 76 of arm circumference put chest at r =
0.84 and MUAC at 0.81, the two highest of any surrogate
([Goto 2011](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12098-010-0288-6)). In 1,640 newborns in Sarlahi, Nepal, chest
circumference below 30.3 cm was 91% sensitive and 83% specific, while foot length reached comparable
sensitivity only once specificity fell below 45%
([Mullany 2007](https://doi.org/10.1038/sj.ejcn.1602504)).

The Indian data are directly usable. In 733 newborns at Ballabgarh, a chest cut-off of 29.5 cm gave
roughly 80% sensitivity and specificity, and a chest-based prediction card worked in both hospital
and community settings ([Kapoor 1996](https://doi.org/10.1136/jech.50.6.683)). In an urban
resettlement colony, chest circumference measured within seven days was the best of four surrogates
at 75.4% sensitivity and 78.4% specificity
([Rustagi 2012](https://doi.org/10.1177/1010539510384717)). Your idea has in fact been built before:
two tricoloured tapes, one for chest and one for mid-arm, were proposed for community health workers
in India in 1988, with chest cut-offs of 30.0 cm for under 2500 g and 27.5 cm for under 2000 g
([Singh 1988](https://doi.org/10.1080/02724936.1988.11748546)).

The counter-argument is practical rather than statistical. A pooled African analysis of 21 studies
found chest at r = 0.79, MUAC at 0.71 and foot length at 0.66, yet recommended foot length and MUAC
as the most feasible, because they do not require undressing the baby and risking cold stress
([Belay 2024](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjpo-2024-002741)). If you are asking a mother on a winter
night in Melghat, that consideration may outrank the accuracy difference.

### Arm circumference

For children aged 6 to 59 months this is settled, and the prior scan covered it. What is new here is
the newborn band: in 268 babies in Navi Mumbai, MUAC cut-offs of 8.6 cm in girls and 8.7 cm in boys
gave 100% sensitivity and negative predictive value for low birth weight, with AUCs of 0.92 and 0.96
([Seth 2021](https://doi.org/10.1111/jpc.15537)). Case 20, the Melghat boy sent home from the
nutrition rehabilitation centre at 6 kg, is the story this measurement is for.

### Abdominal girth and fundal height

Abdominal girth combined with fundal height is the obvious-seeming way to estimate fetal size without
a scan, and it does not work for the babies you care about. In 153 term pregnancies in Keffi,
Nigeria, the product of symphysio-fundal height and abdominal girth correctly predicted 66.2% of
normal-weight babies and 85.7% of macrosomic babies, but **none of the low-birth-weight babies**
([Ariyo 2020](https://doi.org/10.4102/phcfm.v12i1.2113)). A method that performs well on the cases
that need no action and fails completely on the cases that do is worse than no method, because it
manufactures reassurance.

Fundal height alone is little better as a screen. A Cochrane review found a single trial of 1,639
women and concluded there was insufficient evidence to determine whether the measurement detects
growth restriction at all ([Robert Peter 2015](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD008136.pub3)). A
meta-analysis of 46 studies, mostly hospital-based and therefore probably optimistic, found
sensitivity of 0.72 for low birth weight and 0.58 for small-for-gestational-age, and stated plainly
that fundal height is unsuitable for primary screening
([Goto 2013](https://doi.org/10.1136/jech-2012-202141)). There is one study of self-measurement: 300
Swedish women took weekly symphysis-fundus measurements from week 25, and the relative growth derived
from them was independent of fetal sex, maternal obesity and parity
([Bergman 2011](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1600-0412.2010.01026.x)). That establishes the measurement
can be self-administered and that a *trend* carries information; it is not a diagnostic accuracy
study and does not license a referral rule. Collect it as her trend, show it to the doctor, and do
not let it trigger anything.

### The rupee note as a scale reference

The principle behind putting a known-size object in the frame is well validated. Photographing a leg
ulcer with a ruler beside it and measuring in ImageJ gave inter-rater ICC of 0.99 across four raters
from a department head to a medical student
([Aragón-Sánchez 2017](https://doi.org/10.1177/1534734617744951)). A smartphone app recovering scale
reached ICC 0.97 to 1.00 including among raters with no wound-care training at all, beating ruler
measurement at 0.92 to 0.97 ([Wang 2017](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0183139)). Clinical
validation in 82 Asian patients across 2,334 images gave inter-rater ICC of 0.799 to 0.919 against a
wound nurse ([Fong 2023](https://doi.org/10.1111/iwj.13918)). A 1 × 1 cm printed grid under a traced
outline beat a commercial device on both accuracy and precision
([Foltynski 2014](https://doi.org/10.1111/aor.12169)).

Two constraints follow. First, every one of those studies measures a roughly flat area on a roughly
flat surface. A limb or chest circumference is neither, and no study surfaced applying
reference-object photogrammetry to infant circumference; that is an unstudied idea, not a validated
one. Second, if you do this, the reference object must be a single specified denomination or a coin,
because note sizes differ between denominations and "a rupee note" is not one length.

The decisive caution is not about measurement error at all. When 523 surgeons made 1,512 paired
assessments of post-operative cases with and without a wound photograph, adding the photograph left
diagnostic accuracy essentially unchanged at 57.1% versus 58.1%, **reduced sensitivity from 0.58 to
0.50**, increased specificity, made surgeons more confident, and made them more likely to undertriage
([Kummerow Broman 2019](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamasurg.2018.3861)). An accurate measurement
extracted from a photograph is not an accurate decision made from a photograph, and the photograph's
main effect was to raise confidence while lowering sensitivity. That is the same failure shape as the
family-minimisation anchoring result, and it is why the engine below refuses to let any image clear a
flag.

### A printed colour reference

Colour, not size, is where a standard printed object has real evidence behind it. The WHO Haemoglobin
Colour Scale pooled 80% sensitivity for anaemia against 52% for clinical assessment across 14 studies,
a significant improvement — but only 57% sensitivity for *severe* anaemia
([Marn 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2214-109X%2816%2900005-X)). In 5,244 patients across four
Indian medical colleges, the colour strip's positive likelihood ratio was 1.2 to 1.7 and a
non-invasive device 1.5, against 3.7 to 5.7 for a cheap digital haemoglobinometer
([Neogi 2016](https://doi.org/10.1136/jclinpath-2015-203135)). A meta-analysis of point-of-care
devices in children found no significant difference between HemoCue and the colour scale, with pooled
sensitivity 79.5% and specificity 90.1%
([Panda 2024](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00431-024-05875-y)). Case 28 — the Gumla ANM estimating
haemoglobin by colour during a six-week strip stock-out, with two women read as mild turning out
severely anaemic — is precisely the 57% figure playing out. A colour reference is a reasonable
stock-out fallback and a bad primary screen.

### Who holds the tape

This is the finding that should change your architecture, and it is the one most likely to be missed.
The same measurement has completely different properties depending on who takes it.

A cluster-randomised trial in rural Nepal gave families a 6.9 cm foot-length card and a phone number
to call. Across 2,022 intervention and 2,432 control pregnancies, the card **misclassified 84.5% of
babies under 2000 g as normal weight**, and the one apparent behavioural effect could not be
attributed to the intervention ([Hodgins 2020](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-020-09317-w)).
Community volunteers using a counselling-card tool in Tanzania achieved only moderate reliability at
kappa 0.53 and systematically underestimated foot length by 0.26 cm, over-referring as a result
([Marchant 2014](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2458-14-859)). Yet mothers in Sidama, Ethiopia, using a
rigid colour-coded *plastic* tool at a 73 mm cut-off reached AUC 0.82 with 86.9% sensitivity and
76.7% specificity, while the same mothers using a *laminated card* reached only AUC 0.75
([Belay 2025](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjpo-2025-003371)). The physical substrate of the tool changed
the diagnostic performance. Measured instead by frontline health workers with a rigid plastic ruler in
rural Sindh, a 7.6 cm cut-off gave 90.3% sensitivity and 81.8% specificity
([Tikmani 2024](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2024-089153)), and a purpose-built colour-coded
calliper designed for Indian PHC workers and ASHAs tied cut-offs of 6.1, 6.8 and 7.3 cm to four
graded actions ([Pratinidhi 2017](https://doi.org/10.4103/ijmr.IJMR_36_14)). Reliability degrades in
a predictable direction too: community-trained anthropometrists in Ethiopia were less reproducible on
MUAC than on height or weight, and error was larger in smaller children
([Ayele 2012](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0030345)).

The practical rule is that provenance must be a first-class field on every measurement, and the
referral rules must consult it. A foot length from an ASHA with a rigid ruler can trigger a referral.
The same number from a mother with a printed card cannot, on current evidence, and should be stored
as context for the doctor.

### Two things not to build

Smartphone video anthropometry for newborns already exists in India and is in your own deck. The
underlying model reports a relative error of 3.9% and mean absolute error of 114.3 g for weight in a
15 MB offline model ([NurtureNet, arXiv:2405.05530](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.05530) — a preprint,
developer-evaluated, with no independent validation), and your Case 26 reports 111 g across 92 ASHAs
at four PHCs in Daman with impact explicitly unmeasured. The gap there is the loop after the
measurement, not the measurement.

The other is the lookup. In Dharavi, moving the WHO weight-for-height tables from paper into a mobile
calculator cut frontline workers' grade-classification error from 5.5% to 0.7% and raised inter-rater
kappa from 0.79 to 0.97 ([Chanani 2016](https://doi.org/10.1177/0379572116657241)). The error was in
the human reading the table, not in the measurement. That is the cheapest high-yield feature in this
entire scan, and it argues for the app always doing the classification and never asking a person to
interpret a number.

## Implications for the engine

Three design rules fall out of the evidence rather than out of taste, and all three are enforced in
code rather than left to prompt text.

1. Fixed rules decide the disposition and a model may only raise it. Consumer LLM triage under-triaged
   52% of gold-standard emergencies, and family minimisation shifted recommendations toward less
   urgent care with an odds ratio of 11.7
   ([Ramaswamy 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-026-04297-7)).
2. A third party may raise a concern but never retract one the patient raised. This is the anchoring
   result above, turned into an invariant.
3. A measurement drives a rule only if the method is validated for whoever took it, and an image may
   raise a flag but never clear one.

One further detail concerns the EPDS. The pooled Indian figures of 88.9% sensitivity and 93.4%
specificity at a threshold of 13 come from self-report studies
([Fellmeth 2021](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12888-021-03190-6)), but in 177 rural South Indian women
self-administered EPDS produced significantly higher scores and higher detected rates than
interviewer-administered ([Badiya 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajp.2021.102890)). A spoken agent
is closer to the interviewer condition, so the engine records the administration mode alongside the
score and the threshold should be re-derived rather than assumed.

## Where this leaves the roadmap

The measurement work worth doing is narrower and duller than it first appears: a chest-and-arm tape
with the classification done in software, MUAC after discharge, opportunistic blood pressure, a
jaundice look on days two to five, and pulse oximetry wherever a child is involved. The abdominal
girth idea should be dropped, fundal height should be demoted to trend context, and the rupee note
should be reconsidered as a colour reference rather than a scale reference, with the severe-anaemia
sensitivity of 57% kept firmly in view.

Everything in this document is screening and referral evidence. Sensitivity figures quoted for
screening thresholds are not diagnostic performance, positive predictive values in community use are
low by construction, and no measurement here should be reported to a family as a result rather than
as a reason to get a test. Decisions about any individual patient belong to a qualified clinician
with the full picture.
